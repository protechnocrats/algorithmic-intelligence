## Findings

1. **The skill's value is judgment, not correctness.** On the held-out fixture, Opus 5.5 without the skill produced correct code in every session. What it did not do is ask before changing concurrency, think about who controls the input, or say how strongly its changes were verified. Those are the things the skill changed, in every run.
2. **The hostile-input work earned its place:** P5 went from 1/3 (old skill) and 0/3 (no skill) to 3/3.
3. **`DISCOVERY.md` and `CORE-TECHNIQUES.md` were never opened.** P1 was meant to need the discovery method, but the model already knows the "maximum subarray with one deletion" DP and labeled it established, correctly. P1 does not test discovery for this model. A real discovery case needs a problem whose best algorithm is not a known exercise.
4. **The write-mode trigger is the weakest link:** it missed 1 time in 3 on Opus (as in round 2) and every time on Haiku. When it loads, it adds the mode statement and the performance decisions; the reference-version tests are something the models already do on their own.
5. **Rule 4's side-effect clause can be misused.** Haiku applied an unrequested behavior change, justified as a bug "fixed as a side effect" of an optimization, for a bug it had not reproduced. The clause should require that the bug be demonstrated by a failing input and that the optimization be chosen for speed alone, with the bug fix incidental; otherwise the change goes under "Behavior changes for review."
6. **The lift holds on a smaller model:** Haiku 0.83 against 0.39, close to Opus's 1.00 against 0.50. Haiku follows the procedural parts less reliably (no commits, P5 in one run of three).
7. **Cost:** a report-flow session costs about 2.9 times the baseline on the held-out fixture ($2.64 against $0.90), and the current skill costs about the same as the old one. Instructions are a small part of it: skill files opened per session stay under about 20,000 tokens, against 2 to 5 million input tokens per session, nearly all from the extra turns spent on tests, benchmarks, and exhaustive checks.

## Harness problems found and fixed during the round

- **Usage limits:** the 5-hour limit hit four times. The harness now stops starting sessions after a usage-limit error and marks the rest "rerun it later"; no partial session was scored.
- **Hanging final test run:** a session cut off mid-apply left a half-written test that hung `npm test` for 10 hours and blocked the queue. The final test run now has a 10-minute timeout.
- **Stalled turn:** an API stream stalled for over an hour, and `timeout` did not stop it because the process ignored the termination signal. Turns are now force-killed 60 seconds after the timeout, and any turn without a final result line is marked "INCOMPLETE, session invalid."

## Limits

Three runs per condition, one agent, two models. The held-out fixture has been seen by the person tuning the skill, though never used to tune it. P1 turned out not to test discovery (finding 3).
