- **Skill:** `evals/full-grading` at `d4acf87`. The skill files are the same as on `main`; the branch adds only harness changes and cases. Installed from a snapshot. With-skill sessions also had the README's recommended `CLAUDE.md` line.

## Validity

**10 of 15 sessions are valid.** Four sessions had 10% or more of their tool calls refused. A fifth (haiku-5 skill) had all 4 of its shell commands refused, so it never ran the tests. It stopped without applying anything and said why, which is what the skill asks for, but the session cannot be scored. `grade.py` now marks a session invalid when more than half of its shell commands were refused, since the 10% gate missed this one; round 8's validity is unchanged by the new gate. Most refusals were `bash /tmp/<script>.sh`. Allowing that is the same as allowing any command, so the list is not widened further. **Next rounds should run in the dev container with `--skip-permissions`.**

## Behavior: what the probe found

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Sessions with a behavior difference from the original | **0 of 4** | **3 of 3** | 0 of 2 | 0 of 1 |
| J12 grouped knapsack fixed (original: over 60 s) | 4 of 4 (0.02 s) | not measurable | 2 of 2 (0.02 s) | 1 of 1 (0.02 s) |

| J1 appointments: 2,000 round trips cut to 2 | 1 of 4 | not measurable | 2 of 2 | 1 of 1 |
| J9 on 60,000 colliding keys (original: 2.6 s) | 4 of 4 safe (45 to 55 ms) | not measurable | 2 of 2 safe (52, 76 ms) | 1 of 1 safe (47 ms) |
| Mean cost | $0.88 | $0.31 | $5.85 | $1.91 |

**Every valid Haiku baseline changed a public signature.** `Ledger.rangeTotals(double[], ...)` became `rangeTotals(long[] amountsCents, ...)`, to make prefix sums exact. Every caller breaks, and amounts that are not whole cents can no longer be passed. This is case J8's floating-point trap, met by changing the contract rather than keeping it. One of the two invalid Haiku baselines did the same. The probe cannot compile against the changed signature, so these sessions have no other case results.

**Haiku with the skill flagged the same item and left it alone.** haiku-2: "B10 (prefix sums in the ledger): not applied. It changes rounding."

**The hostile-input trap again:** the one Haiku baseline whose code compiled (haiku-3, invalid on refusals) made J9 3.9 times slower under attack: 2.7 s to 10.4 s on colliding keys. Round 7 found the same pattern. No skill session did this.

**The new cases:**
- J12 and J13 were fixed with results unchanged in every session where the probe could run.
- J11 kept its uniform timing in every session.
- Neither J12 nor J13 separates the conditions: Opus without the skill solved both, and so did Haiku with it.

**Where the skill held back a speedup:** 3 of 4 Haiku skill sessions left J1's 2,000 round trips as they were. They listed batching as a behavior change: haiku-4 built it, and its differential test found that a failure of the single-patient call now surfaces differently (9 of 400 cases). The answer key's Pass line asks for the batched version, so these count as speedups held back, the same pattern as RB1 and RB2 in round 8. Whether haiku-4's difference matters depends on whether the single-patient call can fail while the batch call works, which the documented contract does not say. Opus with the skill batched it in both valid sessions.
