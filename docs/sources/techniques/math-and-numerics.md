# Sources for `skills/algorithmic-intelligence/references/techniques/math-and-numerics.md`

Where each claim in that file comes from, for maintainers and reviewers. Not part of the installed skill.

- Donald E. Knuth, *The Art of Computer Programming*, Vol. 2: Seminumerical Algorithms, 3rd ed. (1997).
- Nicholas J. Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, 2nd ed. (2002): compensated and pairwise summation error bounds, Horner's method, factorizations.
- Arnold Neumaier, "Rundungsfehleranalyse einiger Verfahren zur Summation endlicher Summen," *Zeitschrift für Angewandte Mathematik und Mechanik* 54(1), 1974 (Neumaier summation); B. P. Welford, "Note on a Method for Calculating Corrected Sums of Squares and Products," *Technometrics* 4(3), 1962; Tony F. Chan, Gene H. Golub, and Randall J. LeVeque, "Algorithms for Computing the Sample Variance: Analysis and Recommendations," *The American Statistician* 37(3), 1983 (one-pass against two-pass accuracy).
- Volker Strassen, "Gaussian Elimination Is Not Optimal," *Numerische Mathematik* 13(4), 1969.
- Deterministic Miller-Rabin base sets for 64-bit integers: Gerhard Jaeschke, "On Strong Pseudoprimes to Several Bases," *Mathematics of Computation* 61(204), 1993; Jan Feitsma and William Galway's list of base-2 strong pseudoprimes below 2⁶⁴ (2010), from which the 64-bit sets are verified; François Arnault, "Rabin-Miller Primality Test: Composite Numbers Which Pass It," *Mathematics of Computation* 64(209), 1995 (composites passing fixed bases).
- Kitamasa's method (reducing xⁿ modulo the characteristic polynomial) is competitive-programming folklore with no standard published source; its correctness follows from polynomial arithmetic in Knuth, Vol. 2, section 4.6.
- Scott Aaronson and Daniel Gottesman, "Improved Simulation of Stabilizer Circuits," *Physical Review A* 70, 2004; Johnnie Gray and Stefanos Kourtis, "Hyper-Optimized Tensor Network Contraction," *Quantum* 5, 2021; Gushu Li, Yufei Ding, and Yuan Xie, "Tackling the Qubit Mapping Problem for NISQ-Era Quantum Devices" (SABRE), ASPLOS 2019.
- Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright, *Numerical Optimization*, 2nd ed. (2006); Yousef Saad, *Iterative Methods for Sparse Linear Systems*, 2nd ed. (2003).
- David Goldberg, "What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic" (1991).
- Martin R. Albrecht, Jake Massimo, Kenneth G. Paterson, and Juraj Somorovsky, "Prime and Prejudice: Primality Testing Under Adversarial Conditions," ACM CCS 2018: composites crafted to pass fixed-base Miller-Rabin tests.

Sparse matrices and scientific computing:

- Timothy A. Davis, *Direct Methods for Sparse Linear Systems*, SIAM (2006): compressed sparse column and triplet formats and their conversion, fill-in, fill-reducing orderings, sparse LU and Cholesky; Timothy A. Davis, Sivasankaran Rajamanickam, and Wissam M. Sid-Lakhdar, "A Survey of Direct Methods for Sparse Linear Systems," *Acta Numerica* 25, 2016 (supernodal and multifrontal methods, pivoting against ordering in LU).
- SciPy documentation, `scipy.sparse.coo_array` (docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.sparse.coo_array.html): COO permits duplicates and converts fast to CSR and CSC, and "duplicate (i,j) entries will be summed together" on conversion by default; COO does not support arithmetic or slicing directly. The `csr_array` and `csc_array` pages state fast row slicing and fast matrix-vector products for CSR, fast column slicing for CSC, and that changes to the sparsity structure are expensive. Implementation behavior of one library, cited as an example.
- Patrick R. Amestoy, Timothy A. Davis, and Iain S. Duff, "An Approximate Minimum Degree Ordering Algorithm," *SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications* 17(4), 1996; Alan George, "Nested Dissection of a Regular Finite Element Mesh," *SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis* 10(2), 1973 (O(n log n) fill and O(n^1.5) operations on two-dimensional grids); George Karypis and Vipin Kumar, "A Fast and High Quality Multilevel Scheme for Partitioning Irregular Graphs," *SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing* 20(1), 1998 (METIS).
- Richard Barrett et al., *Templates for the Solution of Linear Systems: Building Blocks for Iterative Methods*, SIAM (1994): choosing among CG, MINRES, GMRES, and BiCGSTAB by matrix type, stopping criteria on the relative residual, preconditioners (Jacobi, incomplete factorizations and their breakdown), and direct against iterative methods.
- Yousef Saad, *Iterative Methods for Sparse Linear Systems* (cited above), chapters 6 (CG convergence in terms of √κ), 10 (ILU and its breakdown); Yousef Saad and Martin H. Schultz, "GMRES: A Generalized Minimal Residual Algorithm for Solving Nonsymmetric Linear Systems," *SIAM Journal on Scientific and Statistical Computing* 7(3), 1986; H. A. van der Vorst, "Bi-CGSTAB," *SIAM Journal on Scientific and Statistical Computing* 13(2), 1992; C. C. Paige and M. A. Saunders, "Solution of Sparse Indefinite Systems of Linear Equations," *SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis* 12(4), 1975 (MINRES).
- Jonathan Richard Shewchuk, "An Introduction to the Conjugate Gradient Method Without the Agonizing Pain" (1994): CG iterations proportional to √κ, and its failure on matrices that are not symmetric positive definite.
- William L. Briggs, Van Emden Henson, and Steve F. McCormick, *A Multigrid Tutorial*, 2nd ed., SIAM (2000): near-linear work for elliptic problems.
- Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms* (cited above), chapter 14 ("Matrix Inversion": solving is preferred to forming the inverse) and chapter 20 (least squares: normal equations against QR, error growth with κ²); E. Anderson et al., *LAPACK Users' Guide*, 3rd ed., SIAM (1999): driver routines and the `INFO` error status.
- Threading of BLAS libraries inside parallel code: the OpenBLAS documentation (`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS`, and its warning about using a multithreaded OpenBLAS from an application's own threads) and Intel oneMKL documentation on threading control. Implementation behavior.
- GPU sparse formats: Nathan Bell and Michael Garland, "Implementing Sparse Matrix-Vector Multiplication on Throughput-Oriented Processors," SC 2009 (ELL padded and stored column-major for coalescing, its blow-up with uneven rows, the hybrid ELL plus COO format, and one-thread-per-row CSR's divergence); Moritz Kreutzer, Georg Hager, Gerhard Wellein, Holger Fehske, and Alan R. Bishop, "A Unified Sparse Matrix Data Format for Efficient General Sparse Matrix-Vector Multiplication on Modern Processors with Wide SIMD Units," *SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing* 36(5), 2014 (SELL-C-σ: chunks of C rows, sorting within windows of σ rows); Weifeng Liu and Brian Vinter, "CSR5: An Efficient Storage Format for Cross-Platform Sparse Matrix-Vector Multiplication," ICS 2015 (insensitive to sparsity structure, low conversion cost from CSR); Duane Merrill and Michael Garland, "Merge-Based Parallel Sparse Matrix-Vector Multiplication," SC 2016 (load-balanced products directly on CSR).
